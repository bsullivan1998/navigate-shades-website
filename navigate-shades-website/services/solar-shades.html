Solar Shades

Overview

What We Offer

Solar shades are the single most practical window treatment investment for South Florida homeowners — and when specified correctly, one of the most beautiful. They are engineered to tame Florida's relentless sun without sacrificing your view, your natural light, or your design aesthetic.

The right solar shade fabric reduces solar heat gain significantly, blocks up to 99% of UV radiation, eliminates glare, and protects your furnishings from fading — all while maintaining a clear connection to the outdoors that waterfront and coastal homeowners demand.

The Details That
Make the Difference

UV & Glare Control

Solar shades block up to 99% of harmful UV radiation and dramatically reduce glare — protecting your furnishings, artwork, and flooring from sun damage while making spaces more comfortable to live in.

View Preservation

Unlike blackout treatments, solar shades maintain your outward view — particularly important for waterfront, water-view, and landscape-facing windows. Openness factor selection determines how much of your view is preserved.

Energy Efficiency

By reducing solar heat gain through your windows, solar shades meaningfully reduce your home's cooling load. For South Florida homeowners, this translates to real reductions in air conditioning costs year-round.

Openness Factor Guidance

We walk you through every openness factor option — 1%, 3%, 5%, 10% — and help you choose the right balance of privacy, view, glare control, and light for every room and exposure in your home.

Motorized Integration

Solar shades are ideal for motorization — program them to lower automatically during peak sun hours and raise at sunset, maximizing both energy savings and comfort without manual intervention.

Coastal Durability

Our solar shade fabrics are selected for salt air resistance, UV stability, and long-term color fastness — critical qualities for coastal Florida homes where inferior fabrics degrade within months.

Common Questions

Everything You Need
to Know

What openness factor should I choose for a waterfront home?

For waterfront homes where preserving the water view is a priority, we typically recommend 5% or 10% openness. For west or south-facing exposures with intense afternoon sun, 1% or 3% provides better glare and heat control. We assess each window individually.

Will solar shades keep my home cooler?

Yes — significantly. Solar shades reduce solar heat gain through glass, which reduces the thermal load on your air conditioning system. The energy savings are particularly meaningful in South Florida where cooling runs year-round.

Can I see out at night with solar shades?

Solar shades provide excellent daytime privacy — people outside cannot easily see in while you can see out. At night this reverses when interior lights are on. For nighttime privacy, we typically recommend pairing solar shades with a secondary blackout or room-darkening shade.
